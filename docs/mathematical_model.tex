\begin{abstract}
This note records the equations and modeling assumptions used by the
standalone compressible exhaust-plume study solver.  The implementation is a
calorically perfect, ideal-gas model that combines one-dimensional nozzle
relations with Prandtl--Meyer expansion fans, oblique shocks, and a
two-dimensional geometric construction of plume zones.  It is intended for
reproducible exploratory studies rather than certification analysis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Assumptions and notation}

The gas is modeled as calorically perfect with constant ratio of specific heats
\(\gamma=C_p/C_v\).  The equation of state and speed of sound are
\begin{equation}
  p = \rho \Rgas T,
  \qquad
  a = \sqrt{\gammaa \frac{p}{\rho}},
  \qquad
  V = \Mach a .
\end{equation}
Here \(p\) is static pressure, \(\rho\) is density, \(T\) is static
temperature, \(a\) is speed of sound, \(V\) is flow speed, and \(\Mach\) is
Mach number.  The code uses SI units: pascals, kelvin, kilograms per cubic
meter, meters, and seconds.  Legacy public flow-state angles are stored in
degrees; corrected shock-validity and geometry interfaces use radians as their
canonical angle units.

\section{Simulation contract}

The public solver is
\begin{verbatim}
calculatePlumeZones(...) -> (zones, details)
\end{verbatim}
It accepts scalar inputs and returns a list of immutable zone records plus
construction diagnostics.  The core function has no implicit defaults: every
argument below is required.

\begin{longtable}{p{0.30\linewidth}p{0.10\linewidth}p{0.14\linewidth}p{0.34\linewidth}}
\hline
Input & Units & Required range & Meaning \\
\hline
\texttt{nozzle\_mach} & -- & \(>1\) & Supersonic Mach number at the nozzle exit. \\
\texttt{nozzle\_total\_temperature} & K & \(>0\) & Exit total (stagnation) temperature \(T_0\). \\
\texttt{nozzle\_total\_pressure} & Pa & \(>0\) & Exit total (stagnation) pressure \(p_0\). \\
\texttt{nozzle\_radius} & m & \(>0\) & Radius used to seed the two-dimensional exit geometry. \\
\texttt{atmospheric\_pressure} & Pa & \(>0\) & Ambient static pressure \(p_a\) used for regime selection and pressure equalization. \\
\texttt{gamma} & -- & \(>1\) & Constant ratio of specific heats \(C_p/C_v\). \\
\texttt{num\_expansion\_lines} & count & \(\geq 2\) & Number of discrete expansion-fan states in each expansion sequence. \\
\texttt{num\_compression\_lines} & count & \(\geq 1\) & Number of discrete compression states in each compression sequence. \\
\texttt{num\_plumes} & count & \(\geq 1\) & Number of plume-construction passes appended to the result. \\
\hline
\end{longtable}

All numeric inputs must be finite.  The command-line wrapper adds a separate
input layer: it accepts geopotential altitude and converts that altitude to
\(p_a\) with the atmosphere model described below, and it accepts \(p_0\) in
atmospheres before converting it to pascals.  Its study defaults are 10 km
altitude, \(69\) atm total pressure, \(2000\) K total temperature, exit Mach
\(4.130\), radius \(1\) m, \(\gamma=1.33\), two expansion lines, one
compression line, and one plume pass.  These wrapper defaults are not defaults
of the core solver.

The separate \texttt{EngineParameters} record is a textbook-oriented helper.
It requires mass flow rate, exit radius, total pressure, total temperature,
\(\gamma\), and molar mass; it derives throat area and exit Mach from those
quantities.  It is not currently an alternate argument type for
\texttt{calculatePlumeZones}.  The nozzle-exit helper uses an explicit gas
constant (R=R_u/W); the legacy total-condition wrapper supplies dry air
explicitly for compatibility.

\subsection{Flow-state and zone outputs}

Every \texttt{FlowState} contains the primitive state

\[
  (\Mach, p, T, \rho, \gamma),
\]

where pressure, temperature, and density are static properties.  It exposes
derived total pressure, total temperature, total density, Mach-line angle,
speed of sound, flow speed, and specific total energy.  A \texttt{ZoneResult}
adds the following metadata:

\begin{longtable}{p{0.30\linewidth}p{0.14\linewidth}p{0.44\linewidth}}
\hline
Field & Units & Meaning \\
\hline
\texttt{plume\_index} & count & One-based construction-pass identifier. \\
\texttt{group\_number} & count & One-based physical phase group. \\
\texttt{group\_index} & count & One-based state index within a phase group. \\
\texttt{label} & -- & Human-readable phase label. \\
\texttt{type} & -- & \texttt{Isentropic}, \texttt{ExpansionFan}, or \texttt{ObliqueShock}. \\
\texttt{beta} & degree & Shock angle; NaN when the zone is not a shock. \\
\texttt{theta} & degree & Flow-turning angle; NaN when not applicable. \\
\texttt{coordinates.corners\_ru} & m & Polygon vertices in the two-dimensional right/up plane. \\
\hline
\end{longtable}

The second return value, \texttt{details}, currently contains
\texttt{points}, a mapping from labels \(A\) through \(K\) to two-dimensional
construction points, and \texttt{plume\_fit}, the coefficient vector for the
quadratic surrogate used during reflected-boundary intersections.  The fit is
an implementation diagnostic rather than a stable geometry interchange
format.  Callers should check coordinates for finite values: intermediate
subdivisions of a compression sequence may currently carry placeholder NaN
polygons even when the terminal zone geometry is finite.

\section{Regimes and phase flow}

The exit state is first calculated from \(M_e,T_0,p_0,\gamma\).  The exit
static pressure is
\begin{equation}
  p_e = p_0
  \left(1+\frac{\gammaa-1}{2}M_e^2\right)^{-\gammaa/(\gammaa-1)}.
\end{equation}
The dimensionless pressure residual is
\begin{equation}
  r_p=\frac{p_e-p_a}{p_a}.
\end{equation}
The new API exposes an explicit regime enum.  When \(|r_p|\leq\epsilon_p\),
the result is \texttt{matched} and no shock cells are constructed.  Positive
and negative residuals are \texttt{underexpanded} and \texttt{overexpanded},
respectively.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item If \(p_e>p_a\), the flow is treated as underexpanded.  The solver
    constructs an initial expansion sequence, reflects that sequence across
    the centerline, then applies compression waves to return approximately to
    ambient pressure.
  \item If \(p_e<p_a\) outside the matching tolerance, the flow is treated as overexpanded.  The solver
    first applies an oblique-shock precursor that raises the pressure toward
    \(p_a\), adds a centerline compression, and feeds the resulting state into
    the same reflected expansion/compression geometry.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Oblique-shock validity}

For an upstream Mach number \(M>1\), the corrected solver uses the residual
\begin{equation}
 f(\beta)=\tan\theta-2\cot\beta\frac{M^2\sin^2\beta-1}
 {M^2(\gamma+\cos 2\beta)+2}.
\end{equation}
The weak and strong branches are solved on bounded intervals separated by the
maximum attached turn.  Their exact zero-turn limits are
\begin{equation}
 \beta_{\mathrm{weak}}(0)=\sin^{-1}(1/M),
 \qquad
 \beta_{\mathrm{strong}}(0)=\pi/2.
\end{equation}
Turns above
\(\theta_{\max}=\max_{\mu<\beta<\pi/2}\theta(M,\beta,\gamma)\)
return a structured detached-shock status.  Target-pressure construction is
performed through \(M_{n1}=M\sin\beta\), with the normal-shock pressure-ratio
limit checked before a state is returned.

The phase sequence is therefore:

\begin{description}
  \item[Exit] Isentropic conversion from total conditions to the nozzle-exit
    static state.
  \item[Expansion] Discrete Prandtl--Meyer states divide the turn required to
    reach the ambient pressure at constant total pressure and temperature.
  \item[Reflection] The expansion characteristics are reflected across the
    centerline and evaluated as a second expansion sequence.
  \item[Compression] Oblique compression waves are divided according to
    \texttt{num\_compression\_lines}; the final state is solved to the ambient
    pressure.
  \item[Centerline] A final compression sequence represents the centerline
    interaction and closes the study geometry.
\end{description}

\texttt{ZoneType} identifies the local physical relation, not the global
regime: an underexpanded or overexpanded result can contain isentropic,
expansion-fan, and oblique-shock zones.  Increasing the line counts changes
the discrete resolution of these phase sequences.  Increasing
\texttt{num\_plumes} appends additional construction passes with successive
one-based indices; it does not yet model coupled flow between separate
nozzles.

\section{Geometry contract}

The solver works in a two-dimensional plane with basis vectors
\[
  \mathbf{e}_{R}=(1,0), \qquad \mathbf{e}_{U}=(0,1),
\]
called right and up by the implementation.  A point is represented as
\(\mathbf{x}=(x_R,x_U)\) in meters.  A characteristic or shock segment is
represented parametrically by
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}(s)=\mathbf{x}_0+s\mathbf{d}.
\end{equation}
For two segments, the intersection parameters satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix}d_{1R} & -d_{2R}\\ d_{1U} & -d_{2U}\end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix}s_1\\s_2\end{bmatrix}
  = \mathbf{x}_{0,2}-\mathbf{x}_{0,1}.
\end{equation}
The corrected implementation evaluates this small system with a direct
two-by-two solve, reports determinant, condition number, forward parameters,
and residual, and does not return a pseudoinverse point for a failed ray.

Reflection across the horizontal centerline uses
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{reflect}}=
  \begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The named construction points have the following roles: \(A\) and \(B\) seed
the outer and centerline edges at the exit; \(C\) and \(D\) mark the first and
last centerline points of the initial expansion; \(E\) is the first reflected
intersection; \(F\) and \(G\) locate the outer reflected-boundary surrogate;
and \(H,I,J,K\) close the compression and centerline regions.  Their exact
locations are generated from Mach-line, shock-angle, and flow-turning angles,
not supplied by the user.

For the overexpanded precursor, the centerline reach uses the shock angle in
radians and the analytic radial-drop relation
\begin{equation}
  \Delta x=\frac{R}{\tan\beta}.
\end{equation}

The outer reflected boundary is approximated by a quadratic fitted through
\(A,F,G\).  The fit and subsequent line intersections are deliberately
identified as a study-model geometry approximation.  The optional visualization
helper revolves a closed two-dimensional boundary around a supplied three-
dimensional axis to create a triangular surface mesh; this is display geometry
only and does not alter the flow state.

For the separate projected-area helper, a frustum-like segment with left and
right radii \(R_L,R_R\), axial height \(H\), and view aspect \(\alpha\) uses
\begin{align}
  \Delta R &= R_L-R_R, \\
  \phi_0 &= \cos^{-1}\!\left(\operatorname{clip}\left[-\frac{\Delta R}{H}\tan\alpha,-1,1\right]\right), \\
  A_{\mathrm{proj}} &= (R_L+R_R)\left[\Delta R\,\sin\alpha\,\phi_0
    + |H|\cos\alpha\,\sin\phi_0\right].
\end{align}
This calculation is downstream visualization/analysis and is not part of the
state solve.

\section{Isentropic relations}

For an isentropic change between a static state and its stagnation state,
\begin{align}
  \frac{T_0}{T} &= 1 + \frac{\gammaa-1}{2}\Mach^2, \\
  \frac{p_0}{p} &=
    \left(1 + \frac{\gammaa-1}{2}\Mach^2\right)^{\gammaa/(\gammaa-1)}, \\
  \frac{\rho_0}{\rho} &=
    \left(1 + \frac{\gammaa-1}{2}\Mach^2\right)^{1/(\gammaa-1)}.
\end{align}
The nozzle-exit helper takes \(p_0\), \(T_0\), \(\Mach\), and \(\gammaa\),
then evaluates these relations to obtain the exit static state.  Its current
gas constant is based on the configured dry-air molar mass.

For a quasi-one-dimensional nozzle, the area--Mach relation is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{\Mach}
  \left[\frac{2}{\gammaa+1}
  \left(1+\frac{\gammaa-1}{2}\Mach^2\right)\right]^{{(\gammaa+1)}/{(2(\gammaa-1))}},
\end{equation}
where \(A^*\) is the sonic throat area.  The study solver uses the
supersonic branch for an exit Mach number greater than one.

\section{Prandtl--Meyer expansion}

For a supersonic expansion, the Prandtl--Meyer function is
\begin{equation}
  \nu(\Mach) =
  \sqrt{\frac{\gammaa+1}{\gammaa-1}}
  \tan^{-1}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\gammaa-1}{\gammaa+1}(\Mach^2-1)}\right)
  - \tan^{-1}\!\left(\sqrt{\Mach^2-1}\right).
\end{equation}
The flow turning angle between two states is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\theta = \nu(\Mach_2)-\nu(\Mach_1).
\end{equation}
The expansion fan preserves stagnation pressure and temperature while changing
the static state according to the isentropic relations.

\section{Normal and oblique shocks}

An oblique shock is reduced to a normal shock using the upstream Mach number
normal to the shock,
\begin{equation}
  \Mach_{n1} = \Mach_1\sin\beta,
\end{equation}
where \(\beta\) is the shock angle.  The normal-shock relations used by the
solver are
\begin{align}
  \frac{p_2}{p_1} &= 1 + \frac{2\gammaa}{\gammaa+1}(\Mach_{n1}^2-1), \\
  \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} &=
    \frac{(\gammaa+1)\Mach_{n1}^2}
         {2+(\gammaa-1)\Mach_{n1}^2}, \\
  \frac{T_2}{T_1} &= \frac{p_2/p_1}{\rho_2/\rho_1}, \\
  \Mach_{n2}^2 &=
    \frac{1+\frac{\gammaa-1}{2}\Mach_{n1}^2}
         {\gammaa\Mach_{n1}^2-\frac{\gammaa-1}{2}}.
\end{align}
The downstream Mach number for the oblique shock is reconstructed as
\begin{equation}
  \Mach_2 = \frac{\Mach_{n2}}{\sin(\beta-\theta)},
\end{equation}
where \(\theta\) is the flow-turning angle.

The shock angle is determined from the \(\theta\)--\(\beta\)--\(\Mach\)
relation,
\begin{equation}
  \tan\theta = 2\cot\beta\,
  \frac{\Mach_1^2\sin^2\beta-1}
       {\Mach_1^2\left(\gammaa+\cos(2\beta)\right)+2}.
\end{equation}
The weak-shock branch is used for ordinary compression-wave construction.

\section{Plume-zone construction}

The solver first constructs the nozzle-exit zone from total conditions.  Let
\(p_e\) be its static pressure and \(p_a\) the atmospheric pressure.

\begin{itemize}
  \item If \(p_e > p_a\), the plume is underexpanded.  The solver constructs
    an expansion-fan sequence, reflects the characteristics across the
    centerline, and adds oblique compression waves to approximately equalize
    the plume pressure with the atmosphere.
  \item If \(p_e \leq p_a\), the plume is treated as overexpanded.  An initial
    pair of oblique-shock precursor zones raises the pressure before the
    reflected expansion/compression construction is applied.
\end{itemize}

The geometric construction uses straight characteristic segments, line
intersections, and a fitted parabola for one reflected slip-line boundary.
Some intermediate regions are intentionally represented as approximate study
zones; the method is not a full viscous or reacting-flow solution.

\section{Atmosphere coupling}

The core solver receives atmospheric pressure directly.  The command-line
runner instead receives geopotential altitude \(h\) and evaluates a layered
standard-atmosphere model before calling the solver.  Its layer bases are

\[
  h_b = (0,11,20,32,47,51,71,84.852)\ {\rm km},
\]

with base temperature, pressure-ratio, and temperature-gradient tables stored
with the package.  Within a layer with base state \((T_b,p_b)\), lapse rate
\(\Lambda\), and height increment \(\Delta h\), the implementation uses
\begin{align}
  T &= T_b + \Lambda\Delta h, \\
  \frac{p}{p_b} &= \begin{cases}
    \left(\dfrac{T_b}{T}\right)^{g/(R\Lambda)}, & \Lambda\ne 0,\\[6pt]
    \exp\left(-\dfrac{g\Delta h}{R T_b}\right), & \Lambda=0,
  \end{cases} \\
  \frac{\rho}{\rho_0} &= \frac{p/p_0}{T/T_0}.
\end{align}

The last equation uses sea-level reference quantities in the implementation.
The supported table range is \(0\) to \(84.852\) km.  An altitude outside that
range is clamped to the nearest table boundary and returned with
\texttt{is\_valid=False}; it is not silently treated as an in-range physical
state.

\section{Thermochemistry scope}

The current solver has one constant-\(\gamma\) ideal-gas state and no species
or reaction state.  It does not yet model exhaust composition, chemical
reaction source terms, dissociation, vibrational energy, or temperature-
dependent specific heats.  Molar mass enters the ideal-gas constant in the
textbook and engine-parameter helpers, but the plume-zone contract does not
accept a species mixture.  The broader thermodynamic helper functions are
retained as reference and cohort material; they are not all claims about the
current plume solver's active physics.

\section{Conservation checks}

The benchmark tests in the repository verify the algebraic relations above.
For a normal shock they additionally check conservation of mass flux,
streamwise momentum flux, and total enthalpy:
\begin{align}
  \rho_1 V_1 &= \rho_2 V_2, \\
  p_1+\rho_1 V_1^2 &= p_2+\rho_2 V_2^2, \\
  C_pT_1+\frac{V_1^2}{2} &= C_pT_2+\frac{V_2^2}{2}.
\end{align}
For isentropic transformations, the tests verify that converting static
properties to stagnation properties and back preserves the supplied state.

\section{Future work: finite plume termination}

The current construction does not contain a physical plume termination
condition.  It repeatedly applies idealized adiabatic and, for the smooth
wave portions, isentropic state changes.  Those relations preserve total
temperature and total pressure through each expansion sequence, while the
geometry builder continues to create another discrete region whenever the
requested construction pass continues.  Consequently, a calculation can
produce a sequence of plausible-looking zones without representing the point
where the exhaust has mixed into the ambient flow.

This is a model boundary, not an iterative solver failure.  Adiabatic
isentropic relations describe local wave transformations; they do not provide
entrainment, diffusion, viscous dissipation, turbulent mixing, or a finite
ambient reservoir by themselves.

Potential termination strategies, in increasing physical complexity, are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Weak-wave cutoff.} Stop when the pressure, Mach, turning-angle,
    and total-pressure changes across the next wave are below prescribed
    tolerances.  This is the smallest change and is useful for controlling
    geometric over-generation, but it is a numerical definition of
    termination rather than a mixing model.
  \item \textbf{Ambient-equilibrium cutoff.} Stop when a zone or cross-section
    is sufficiently close to the ambient pressure, temperature, density, and
    velocity, using dimensionless residuals and a minimum persistence length.
    The persistence requirement prevents one local crossing of ambient
    pressure from terminating the plume prematurely.
  \item \textbf{Finite-domain cutoff.} Define a maximum axial distance or
    radius of interest and report the plume state at that boundary.  This is
    appropriate when the study asks for a bounded observation volume, but the
    boundary must be recorded as a truncation rather than physical
    equilibration.
  \item \textbf{Entrainment and mixing model.} Add a shear-layer or integral
    plume model that exchanges mass, momentum, and energy with the ambient.
    The plume can then terminate when its cross-section and conserved fluxes
    approach the ambient mixture within specified tolerances.
  \item \textbf{Higher-fidelity continuation.} Couple the near-field zone
    construction to a viscous, turbulent, multispecies, or reacting-flow
    solver.  This would introduce the mechanisms that the current
    constant-\(\gamma\) model intentionally omits.
\end{enumerate}

Before implementation, the project should choose whether plume termination
means weak waves, thermodynamic equilibrium, a finite study domain, or a
validated downstream coupling boundary.  The eventual result contract should
also report a termination reason, the final residuals to ambient, the last
active wave type, and whether the result ended by physical criterion or by
domain truncation.  Until that policy is chosen, \texttt{num\_plumes} and the
line-count inputs should be understood as construction controls, not as a
prediction of plume lifetime.

\section{Known limits}

The reflected geometry can become ill-conditioned for extreme pressure ratios
and very low or very high Mach numbers.  The current implementation reports
invalid input values early, but it does not claim convergence for every
physically imaginable input.  Future work should replace the geometric hacks
with explicit characteristic validity checks and report a structured solver
status alongside the zone list.
