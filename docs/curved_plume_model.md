# Curved Exhaust-Plume Integral Model

## Status

This note defines the first implementation increment for rotor-wash- and crossflow-deflected exhaust plumes. The implemented kernel is deliberately narrower than the full research model:

- steady, slender, continuously emitted plume;
- three-dimensional centerline parameterized by arc length;
- locally pressure-matched flow;
- top-hat equivalent circular cross-section;
- inert source-origin tracer;
- calorically perfect two-stream mixing;
- curvature generated by momentum imported with entrained ambient flow;
- an explicit external force/energy source-term interface whose default closure is zero;
- no enabled buoyancy, form drag, added mass, reactions, radiation loss, wall impingement, rotor model, or passive-cloud handoff yet.

The omitted physics is not hidden inside fitted coefficients. Force and energy effects must enter through the source-term interface; mixing behavior must enter through an entrainment or thermodynamic closure.

## 1. Conserved state

Let the centerline position be \(\mathbf r(s)\), where \(s\) is centerline arc length. The numerical state is

\[
\mathbf q(s)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf r & \dot m & \mathbf P & \dot{\mathcal H} & \dot m_e
\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T},
\]

where

- \(\dot m\) is total plume mass flow;
- \(\mathbf P=\dot m\mathbf V\) is vector momentum flux;
- \(\dot{\mathcal H}=\dot m(h+V^2/2)\) is total-energy flux;
- \(\dot m_e=\dot mY_e\) is conserved exhaust-origin tracer flow.

The tangent follows the momentum direction:

\[
\mathbf t=\frac{\mathbf P}{\lVert\mathbf P\rVert}
          =\frac{\mathbf V}{V}.
\]

## 2. Version-0 conservation equations

For ambient velocity \(\mathbf U_a(\mathbf r)\), entrained mass per unit plume length \(\mathcal E_m\), external force per unit length \(\mathbf F'\), and external energy source per unit length \(\dot q'\),

\[
\frac{d\mathbf r}{ds}=\mathbf t,
\]

\[
\frac{d\dot m}{ds}=\mathcal E_m,
\]

\[
\frac{d\mathbf P}{ds}=\mathcal E_m\mathbf U_a+\mathbf F',
\]

\[
\frac{d\dot{\mathcal H}}{ds}
=\mathcal E_m\left(h_a+\frac12U_a^2\right)+\dot q',
\]

\[
\frac{d\dot m_e}{ds}=0.
\]

With the default source-term closure \(\mathbf F'=\mathbf 0\) and \(\dot q'=0\), these equations preserve three useful invariants in a uniform ambient field:

\[
\mathbf P-\dot m\mathbf U_a=\text{constant},
\]

\[
\dot{\mathcal H}
-\dot m\left(h_a+\frac12U_a^2\right)
=\text{constant},
\]

\[
\dot mY_e=\text{constant}.
\]

The first invariant is the mechanism that bends the plume: as ambient mass enters, its pre-existing vector momentum enters with it.

## 3. Thermodynamic reconstruction

For the current two-stream calorically perfect closure, define the source-origin fraction

\[
f_s=\frac{\dot m_0}{\dot m}.
\]

Then

\[
c_{p,m}=f_sc_{p,0}+(1-f_s)c_{p,a},
\]

\[
R_m=f_sR_0+(1-f_s)R_a.
\]

From the conserved energy flux,

\[
T=\frac{\dot{\mathcal H}/\dot m-V^2/2}{c_{p,m}}.
\]

Pressure is closed with the local ambient pressure,

\[
p=p_a(\mathbf r),
\]

and ideal-gas density is

\[
\rho=\frac{p_a}{R_mT}.
\]

The equivalent top-hat area and radius follow from continuity:

\[
A=\frac{\dot m}{\rho V},
\qquad
b=\sqrt{\frac{A}{\pi}}.
\]

A constant-density thermodynamic closure is included only for exact analytical regression tests.

## 4. Candidate entrainment closure

The conservation kernel accepts any nonnegative entrainment model. The first candidate separates axial shear and normal crossflow contributions.

With

\[
W_\parallel=
\left|\left(\mathbf V-\mathbf U_a\right)\cdot\mathbf t\right|,
\]

and

\[
U_\perp=
\left\|\mathbf U_a-
(\mathbf U_a\cdot\mathbf t)\mathbf t\right\|,
\]

use

\[
\mathcal E_s=
2\pi b\,\alpha_s f_{\mathrm{dev}}
\sqrt{\rho\rho_a}\,W_\parallel,
\]

\[
\mathcal E_f=
2b\,C_f\rho_aU_\perp.
\]

The developing-jet factor is

\[
f_{\mathrm{dev}}(s)=
 f_0+(1-f_0)\left[1-e^{-s/L_{\mathrm{dev}}}\right].
\]

The combination rule remains exposed as a generalized mean,

\[
\mathcal E_m=
\left(\mathcal E_s^{p_e}+\mathcal E_f^{p_e}\right)^{1/p_e},
\qquad p_e\ge 1.
\]

Neither \(C_f\) nor \(p_e\) is considered validated. They must be calibrated against jet-in-crossflow trajectory and dilution data after the free-jet shear model is fixed.

## 5. Curvature diagnostic

For the general source-term interface,

\[
\frac{d\mathbf t}{ds}
=\frac{1}{\lVert\mathbf P\rVert}
\left(\mathbf I-\mathbf t\mathbf t^{\mathsf T}\right)
\left(\mathcal E_m\mathbf U_a+\mathbf F'\right).
\]

The code evaluates curvature from this full momentum derivative. With the default zero-force closure,

\[
\kappa=
\left\|\frac{d\mathbf t}{ds}\right\|
=\frac{\mathcal E_mU_\perp}{\dot mV}.
\]

The code reports both \(\kappa\) and the local slenderness indicator

\[
\epsilon_\kappa=\kappa b.
\]

Large \(\epsilon_\kappa\) indicates that the one-dimensional swept-tube approximation is losing validity.

## 6. Exact regression solution: constant-density free jet

For quiescent ambient flow, equal constant density, no forced entrainment, and

\[
\mathcal E_m=2\pi b\rho\alpha V,
\]

momentum is constant. If the source radius and speed are \(b_0\) and \(V_0\), then

\[
\dot m(s)=\dot m_0\left(1+\frac{2\alpha s}{b_0}\right),
\]

\[
b(s)=b_0+2\alpha s,
\]

\[
V(s)=\frac{V_0}{1+2\alpha s/b_0},
\]

\[
Y_e(s)=\frac{Y_{e,0}}{1+2\alpha s/b_0}.
\]

For equal constant specific heats,

\[
T(s)=T_a+
\frac{T_0-T_a+V_0^2/(2c_p)}{1+2\alpha s/b_0}
-\frac{V(s)^2}{2c_p}.
\]

This solution tests mass growth, radius growth, momentum conservation, energy accounting, cooling, and tracer dilution together.

## 7. Exact regression solution: orthogonal uniform crossflow

Let the source velocity be \(V_j\mathbf e_j\), the uniform ambient velocity be \(U_c\mathbf e_c\), and \(\mathbf e_j\cdot\mathbf e_c=0\). For constant entrainment \(\mathcal E_0\),

\[
\dot m=\dot m_0+\mathcal E_0s,
\]

\[
\mathbf P=\dot m_0V_j\mathbf e_j
+\mathcal E_0sU_c\mathbf e_c.
\]

Define the turning length

\[
L=\frac{\dot m_0V_j}{\mathcal E_0U_c}.
\]

The exact displacements are

\[
x_c(s)=\sqrt{s^2+L^2}-L,
\]

\[
x_j(s)=L\,\operatorname{asinh}(s/L).
\]

Hence

\[
\mathbf r(s)=\mathbf r_0+x_c\mathbf e_c+x_j\mathbf e_j.
\]

The initial curvature is

\[
\kappa_0=\frac{1}{L}
=\frac{\mathcal E_0U_c}{\dot m_0V_j}.
\]

This solution tests the three-dimensional centerline integration independently of any empirical entrainment law.

## 8. Implemented acceptance tests

The first increment requires all of the following:

1. Numerical free-jet histories match the closed-form solution.
2. Numerical uniform-crossflow positions and velocities match the closed-form solution.
3. Uniform-ambient relative momentum, relative energy, and exhaust tracer remain invariant.
4. Rotating the full source and ambient problem rotates the answer without changing scalar histories.
5. A prescribed external force and energy source produce the exact momentum and energy-flux changes and the expected initial curvature.
6. A source outside the documented pressure-match tolerance fails before integration.

## 9. Next implementation increments

### Increment 2: ambient-field composition

Add composable velocity fields for freestream, main rotor, tail rotor, and imported CFD grids. Thermodynamic ambient properties should remain a separate background field so velocity superposition cannot accidentally add pressure or temperature.

### Increment 3: actuator-disk rotor field

Implement a bounded steady engineering field with thrust-derived axial wash, optional torque-derived swirl, radial loading, wake contraction, and a clearly documented hover/low-advance-ratio validity range.

### Increment 4: buoyancy and external interactions

Use the implemented source-term interface to add buoyancy only after deriving and testing its pressure-gradient, enthalpy, and potential-energy bookkeeping. Form drag and added mass must remain separate calibratable models.

### Increment 5: developing and crossflow calibration

Calibrate \(\alpha_s\), \(f_0\), and \(L_{\mathrm{dev}}\) against round free-jet data first. Only then fit \(C_f\) and \(p_e\) against uniform crossflow trajectory and dilution data.

### Increment 6: noncircular sections and swept geometry

Replace the circular area closure with ellipse or sheet models that expose perimeter and projected crossflow width. Generate the mesh with a rotation-minimizing frame rather than by revolving a fixed two-dimensional boundary.

### Increment 7: coherent-jet handoff

When relative velocity becomes small while temperature or tracer anomaly remains important, transition to an advected scalar-cloud model rather than extending coherent-jet equations indefinitely.

### Increment 8: infrared state

Add species mass fractions and independent thermal/scalar widths. Export centerline, orientation, cross-section, temperature, pressure, density, and species fields for curved-plume ray intersection and spectral radiative transfer.
